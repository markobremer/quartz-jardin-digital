#infancia #juego 

> On the surface, it’s not clear why a need for risky play would evolve across species, if it increases the chances that something bad will happen to those who partake in it. But when we dig a little deeper, its benefits become obvious. Risky play provides children with low-cost opportunities to develop the physical and cognitive skills to master the challenges that they will face as they grow older. So, those who engaged in it had a major [evolutionary advantage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/147470491100900212) over those who did not. Physically, risky play allows children to explore more diverse movements and gain physical movement skills. [Cognitively](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21594937.2022.2152531), it helps them overcome their fears, build their critical thinking skills, and become accustomed to coping independently with difficult situations.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqs6cwa1r4jvwq0t9svnnd4q))
