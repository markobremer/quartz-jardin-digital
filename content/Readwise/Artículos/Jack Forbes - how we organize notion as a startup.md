#

> In the 6 months we've now been using Notion, I've found we genuinely **seek** topics to write a Notion page about, which is great when documentation is so vitally important for us as a remote company.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwdahv3v2j6r5wc45spr349h))

> The proper use of databases in your company's docs is crucial. The same way a software company should build its codebase for the future, you need to build your company documents for the future.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwdamhsxx0c62dskg3s3b9cb))

> Notion has the amazingly undervalued feature that is [linked databases](https://www.notion.so/help/linked-databases). Linked databases let you show, filter, and sort the same source data across multiple locations.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwdan522wyszfqhnhd74yrh7))

> Documentation is [vital for fully-remote companies](https://about.gitlab.com/company/culture/all-remote/tips/). Finding that balance of trying every great new tool without wasting time and overwhelming our team is tough.
> **My major takeaway:** Notion has unlocked [a world of possibility](https://www.notion.so/Notion-Template-Gallery-181e961aeb5c4ee6915307c0dfd5156d) and for companies, the rule of thumb is: **databaseify**, **templateify**, and **wikify** ALL THE THINGS.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwdadgrsqbkv0wzye8crbn48))

> Don't get me wrong, I love [Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/) (formerly known as G Suite). We still use Google Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, etc. It's just that when it comes to company documentation, I feel icky adding new GDocs to our Drive because it feels like more clutter and stuff I'll never look at again. Then we can never find what we're looking for and ultimately have to ask our teammates.
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hwdaegwpzp1g1gaawh7dr69s))
