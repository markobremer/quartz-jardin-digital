> Economic “agents” maximize utility under constraint. Sutton and Barto, fathers of reinforcement learning, turn utility into “reward,” giving [examples](https://substack.com/redirect/ade15b08-3df4-4506-82ed-152e66b7cf4e?j=eyJ1IjoiM2c2ZWE0In0.UBnO9epFHJI2gArKgnhPNpI_1rjXwO8S1S6smvAQsPI) of a chess player making a move or a cleaning robot optimizing its path. In each case, an agent “seeks to achieve a goal *despite* uncertainty about its environment.”
 ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01k5rh7dg19fppk6wav1vbdxj8))



#nota 

> While a pocket of tech elites have been using “high-agency” since the mid-2010s, it’s no surprise the term has taken off amid the LLM boom. Given access to a system that’s memorized all documented human knowledge, what matters is not expertise, but a dogged ability to adapt and win no matter who you are and what you start with.
