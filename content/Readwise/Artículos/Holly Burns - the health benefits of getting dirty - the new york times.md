> Research has suggested that people who grow up on farms, for instance, have lower rates of [Crohn’s disease](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article-abstract/120/2/354/70454/Contact-With-Farm-Animals-in-Early-Life-and?autologincheck=redirected), [asthma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5682224/#:~:text=None%20of%20the%20Amish%20children,studies%20%5B61%2C62%5D) and [allergies](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pai.13807), likely because of their exposure to a diverse array of microbes.

> A recent [Finnish experiment](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aba2578) found that children attending urban day cares where a native “forest floor” had been planted had both a stronger immune system and a healthier microbiome than those attending day cares with gravel yards — and [continued to have](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34403882/) beneficial gut and skin bacteria two years later.
