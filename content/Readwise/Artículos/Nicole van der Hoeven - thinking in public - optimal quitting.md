#

> Why don't we quit earlier?
> It's not just social conditioning-- there are a lot of [cognitive biases](https://amzn.to/4803gBj?utm_source=nvdh&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=thinking-in-public-optimal-quitting-02) at play here. We irrationally take into account the effort we've already expended in the past when making projections about the future ([*sunk cost fallacy*](https://time.com/5347133/sunk-cost-fallacy-decisions/?utm_source=nvdh&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=thinking-in-public-optimal-quitting-02)). We don't take into account opportunity costs. We place undue importance on things that we've built ([*the Ikea effect*](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/ikea-effect?utm_source=nvdh&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=thinking-in-public-optimal-quitting-02))-- one of the reasons why developers shouldn't be the only ones testing their code. We work so long that we experience an [enmeshment](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/contemporary-psychoanalysis-in-action/201410/tangled-in-enmeshment?utm_source=nvdh&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=thinking-in-public-optimal-quitting-02) of our jobs with our identities: what we do becomes who we are... and how can we quit who we are? And finally, we quit less frequently and more belatedly when we're being observed.
