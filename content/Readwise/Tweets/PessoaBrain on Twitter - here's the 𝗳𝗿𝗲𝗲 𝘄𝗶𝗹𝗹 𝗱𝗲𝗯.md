> Sapolsky's argument is very poor, basically the argument that if everything depends on all factors from the immediate past, iterating the argument you end up with no causal power (no uncaused causes in a physical universe): "you're not free because you were caused by something"
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/PessoaBrain/status/1725905147790815493))



#determinismo #crítica #causalidad #agencia 

> I'm no expert in theological studies, but the argument seems strikingly close to the notion of an "unmoved mover" by Aquinas. By tracing the chain backwards, everything moves because of an initial mover, which was obviously god according to Aquinas.
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/PessoaBrain/status/1725905149539840396))

> Sapolsky's version is the same except that it's all material causes going backwards in time. So I'm not free because everything depends on past causes, ad infinitum. Seems like an extremely weak form of reasoning in 2023.
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/PessoaBrain/status/1725905151133724973))



#agencia #crítica 

> Here's the truly disappointing thing: the admission by Sapolsky that the argument is ideological/political. That is just nonsense. One doesn't need neuroscience, evolution, biology to argue in favor of positions about society, justice, etc. That's a complete misuse of science!
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/PessoaBrain/status/1725905152761020422))

> On top of that you don't build arguments based on very weak evidence from social psychology experiments that have very small effects sizes (and replicability!) and antiquated notions that the brain is controlled by a prefrontal cortex (a homunculus controlling behavior??).
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/PessoaBrain/status/1725905154325549488))

> In any case that's my take. Even if you're not into the "free will" thing (like me!), Kevin's book does a beautiful job at discussing how the brain+evolution brings about agency to animals. To me that was the most satisfying part of the book, filling a very important space
