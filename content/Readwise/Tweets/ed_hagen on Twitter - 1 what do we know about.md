#

> 1. What do we know about hunter-gatherers (HG)? This is the go-to book, first published in 1995, w/ a 2nd edition in 2013. Here's an overview of the book that I hope will encourage <a href="https://twitter.com/NPR">@NPR</a>, <a href="https://twitter.com/sciam">@sciam</a>, & others to consult it when reporting on & evaluating the import of new HG studies 🧵 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJgtB-bIAAz6F1.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080063546376232))

> 2. The key theme is that HGs vary a lot, hence the word *Spectrum* in the subtitle, but in principled ways that are best understood in a human behavioral ecology (HBE) framework: 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJhGcYa4AAMCzW.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080065672884364))

> 3. The Preface recounts the sensationalist "discovery" of the Tasady, widely reported in the media as Stone Age relics, which was later debunked. A cautionary tale: 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJhZM5a4AAr8ST.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080068353134825))

> 4. The diverse geographic locations of the HG societies discussed in the text: 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJhz_1bMAAWAdl.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080070601224514))

> 5. Chapter 1: Discusses the role of HGs in Anthro & 20th century thought, highlighting how the 1966 Man the Hunter conference overturned Man the Hunter! This is (or should be) known to Anth 101 students, yet is still misreported by the media & even recent scientific publications 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJiFR7aQAAgFio.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080074237648903))

> 6. Chapter 2: Reviews the different theoretical frameworks that have been applied to HGs. Kelly picks HBE, but states "you will have to judge for yourself through the succeeding chapters whether or not it suits your needs" 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJidG_boAALvhJ.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080076632683000))

> 7. Chapter 3: Foraging & Subsistence, the 1966 MtH conference, optimal foraging theory, & dietary variation. It concludes that the "hunter-gatherer diet cannot be stereotyped and...is related in a fairly straightforward fashion to gross environmental characteristics" 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJiwGQbAAA4Wwz.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080079065362851))

> 8. Chapter 4: Mobility, a defining feature of many HG societies. The often substantial costs of moving must be outweighed by the benefits. Mobility has a major impact on other dimensions of HG life (eg, women pulling the sleds!), as discussed in subsequent chapters. 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJjNuxbIAAISWr.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080081388962283))

> 9. Chapter 5: Technology. The tradeoffs of investing time and energy into the manufacture and transport of simple or complex technology vs. the benefits gained from it: 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJjsEubEAAuvXG.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080084002013185))

> 10. Ch 6: Sharing, Exchange, & Land Tenure. An ethic of sharing is fundamental in many HGs. Kelly concludes "There are costs & benefits to sharing resources, & it is clear that HGs balance these when making decisions to share food or to admit outsiders into their territory." 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJkA2zaQAAtnTV.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080087328124980))

> 11. Ch 7: Group Size and Demography. Optimal group sizes, population growth rates, carrying capacity, mortality, including from violence and warfare, fertility, infanticide, birth spacing, maternal condition, and more... 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJkQ5Ub0AA9OYv.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080090926862847))

> 12. Ch 8: Men, Women, and Foraging. The juiciest chapter? Egalitarianism, variability in postmarital residence, descent, marriage, and the division of labor. Women hunt! But the real division of labor is not hunting vs. gathering but risky vs. non-risky foraging: 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJks7fbYAEI7pS.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080093363777611))

> 13. Ch 9: Non-egalitarian hunter gatherers. HGs exhibit dramatic variation in social complexity. 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJlDohasAAUqAB.jpg)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080095993610720))

> 14. Ch 10: Hunter gatherers and Prehistory. "misuse of modern hunter-gatherer research provides spurious support for the idea of a single primitive human society, a uniform hunter-gatherer stage" 
> ![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GGJlNB7aoAAGA-v.png)
 ([View Tweet](https://twitter.com/ed_hagen/status/1757080098870837252))
